Online shopping scams follow a handful of repeating patterns. Recognizing them makes it much
easier to shop safely.

## Too-good-to-be-true discount stores

A store advertises designer or branded products at 80-90% off, often through a social media ad
with a countdown timer. Orders either never arrive or arrive as cheap counterfeits.

## Non-delivery scams

You pay for an item that's never shipped. These stores are often built quickly, run for a short
window, and disappear once complaints accumulate.

## Counterfeit goods

Products arrive, but they're low-quality counterfeits rather than the genuine branded item
advertised — common on marketplaces with lower seller verification standards.

## Fake marketplace listings

A legitimate-looking listing on a real marketplace turns out to be from a fraudulent seller,
sometimes directing you to pay outside the platform's protected checkout — a major warning sign,
since it removes any buyer protection the platform would normally offer.

## Subscription traps

A "free trial" or "just pay shipping" offer quietly enrolls you in a recurring subscription
that's difficult to cancel.

## How to shop safely

- Check the return/refund policy and contact information before buying.
- Search the store name plus "reviews" or "scam" before purchasing from an unfamiliar store.
- Pay with a credit card or protected payment method, not a direct bank transfer.
- Never pay for a marketplace item outside the platform's official checkout.
- Read the fine print before agreeing to any "free trial" offer.
