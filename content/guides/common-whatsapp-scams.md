WhatsApp's popularity and personal feel make it a common channel for scams. Here are the
patterns reported most often.

## The "wrong number" scam

A friendly message arrives claiming to be a wrong number, then builds a rapport over days before
introducing an investment "opportunity." This is often the opening move of a longer-running
investment or romance scam. Be cautious of unsolicited friendly contact that gradually shifts
toward money or investing.

## Fake job offers

Messages offering easy, high-paying work (often "product boosting" or "task-based" jobs)
recruited entirely through WhatsApp, sometimes asking for an upfront "registration" payment. See
our full guide: [How to Recognize a Job Scam](/guides/how-to-recognize-a-job-scam).

## Verification code theft

A message claims to be from WhatsApp support, a friend, or a business, asking you to forward a
6-digit code you just received by SMS. This code is what lets someone else register your phone
number on their device — never share it with anyone.

## "Hi Mum/Dad" scams

A message claims to be from a family member texting from a new number, often followed by an
urgent request for money to cover an emergency. Verify by calling the family member directly
using the number you already have for them.

## Fake prize or reward messages

Messages claiming you've won a prize from a well-known brand, asking you to click a link and
enter personal details or pay a small "delivery fee" to claim it.

## How to protect yourself

- Never share a verification code, even with someone claiming to be a friend or official support.
- Verify unexpected requests for money through a separate channel, like a phone call.
- Don't click unfamiliar links, especially shortened ones.
