Checking whether a website is legitimate takes only a few minutes once you know what to look
for. Here's a practical process you can run through before entering any personal or payment
information.

## 1. Look closely at the domain name

Scam sites often use a domain that's close to, but not exactly, a well-known brand's real
domain — an extra word, a hyphen, or a different ending (`.shop` instead of `.com`, for
example). Read the domain character by character rather than skimming it.

## 2. Check for HTTPS

Click the padlock icon (or look for `https://` at the start of the address) before entering any
information. This isn't proof of legitimacy on its own, but its absence on a site handling
payments or logins is a real red flag.

## 3. Look for real contact information

Scroll to the footer or an "About" / "Contact" page. Legitimate businesses are usually easy to
reach — a working support email, and ideally a phone number or physical address. Vague or
missing contact details are a warning sign.

## 4. Read the return and refund policy

A real online store will have a clear, specific policy on returns and refunds. If it's missing,
vague, or copy-pasted with obvious errors, be cautious.

## 5. Search for independent reviews

Search the site's name plus "reviews" or "scam" on a search engine. Look past the first result —
check forums, review aggregators, and social media for real customer experiences, not just
testimonials shown on the site itself.

## 6. Notice pressure tactics

Countdown timers, "only 2 left in stock," and urgent pop-ups are designed to stop you from
thinking it through. Genuine urgency (like a real flash sale) is rare; constant urgency on every
page is a pattern worth noticing.

## What to do if something feels off

If you're unsure after going through this checklist, don't enter payment details. Instead,
search for the company through a trusted source (like a well-known price comparison site) or ask
someone you trust to take a look before you proceed.
