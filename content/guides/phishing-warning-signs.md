Phishing is an attempt to trick you into handing over sensitive information — passwords,
verification codes, or payment details — by impersonating a trusted brand, service, or person.
Here's a complete checklist of the warning signs to watch for, across any channel.

## Sender / source signals

- The sender address doesn't match the company it claims to represent
- A generic greeting ("Dear Customer") instead of your actual name
- The message arrives from an unexpected channel for that type of communication (e.g. your
  "bank" texting you instead of using their app's official notifications)

## Language signals

- Urgency: "act now," "your account will be suspended," "verify immediately"
- Threats: legal action, arrest, account deletion
- Unusual requests: passwords, one-time codes, full card numbers

## Link signals

- The visible link text doesn't match where it actually leads (hover to check)
- A shortened link hiding the true destination
- A domain that's close to, but not exactly, the real company's domain

## What to do when you spot these signs

1. Don't click the link or reply.
2. If you're worried the issue might be real, go to the official site or app directly instead of
   using the link provided.
3. Report and delete the message.
4. If you already entered credentials, change that password immediately and enable two-factor
   authentication.
