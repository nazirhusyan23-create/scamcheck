Phishing emails aim to steal your login details or trick you into an unwanted payment by
impersonating a company, coworker, or service you trust. Here's how to spot one.

## Check the sender address, not just the name

Email clients display a name ("Amazon Support") that can be set to anything. What matters is the
actual email address behind it. A message claiming to be from a well-known company but sent from
an unrelated or misspelled domain is a strong sign of phishing.

## Look for urgency and threats

Phishing emails frequently use language like:

- "Your account will be suspended within 24 hours"
- "Unusual sign-in activity detected — verify now"
- "Final notice: action required"

Legitimate account issues are rarely this dramatic, and companies typically give you more than a
few hours to respond.

## Hover before you click

Hover your mouse over any link (without clicking) to see the actual destination URL, usually
shown in the bottom corner of your browser or email client. If the link text says one thing but
the real address is completely different, don't click it.

## Never enter your password after clicking an email link

If an email asks you to "verify your account," go to the official website directly by typing the
address yourself or using a bookmark — not by clicking the link in the email.

## Watch for generic greetings

"Dear Customer" or "Dear User" instead of your actual name can indicate a mass-sent phishing
email, though some legitimate automated emails also use generic greetings, so treat this as one
signal among several, not proof on its own.

## Be wary of unexpected attachments

An unexpected invoice, shipping label, or "document" attachment — especially from an unfamiliar
sender — is a common way to deliver malware. Don't open attachments you weren't expecting.
