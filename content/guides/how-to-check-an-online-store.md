Fake online stores are often built quickly around a single viral product or an unrealistic
discount, then disappear after collecting a batch of payments. Here's a quick pre-purchase
checklist.

## 1. Check the return and refund policy

A clear, specific return policy is a good sign. A vague, missing, or copy-pasted policy full of
errors is not.

## 2. Look for real contact information

A working support email is the bare minimum — a phone number or physical address adds more
confidence.

## 3. Be skeptical of extreme discounts

An 80-90% discount storewide, especially combined with urgency ("today only!"), is a common
pattern in fake stores designed to move quickly before complaints pile up.

## 4. Search for independent reviews

Look beyond the testimonials shown on the site itself — search the store name plus "reviews" or
"scam" to see what other shoppers have reported elsewhere.

## 5. Check how long the site has existed

A store that appears to have launched only weeks ago, with little to no online footprint, is
riskier than an established retailer — though every legitimate store was new once, so weigh this
alongside the other signals.

## 6. Use a payment method with buyer protection

Pay by credit card or a trusted payment processor that lets you dispute a charge, rather than a
direct bank transfer, especially for a store you haven't used before.

## If you already paid and suspect a scam

Contact your card issuer or payment provider immediately to ask about a dispute or chargeback,
and keep any order confirmation emails as evidence.
