Payment scams take many forms, but most share a common structure: urgency, an unusual payment
method, and pressure not to double-check.

## Advance-fee scams

You're told you need to pay a small fee to unlock a much larger sum — a prize, inheritance,
loan, or refund. Legitimate prizes and refunds don't require you to pay money first.

## Gift card payment requests

Being asked to pay a bill, fine, fee, or "debt" using gift cards is one of the clearest scam
signals. No real company, government agency, or law-enforcement body accepts gift cards as
payment.

## Fake invoices

An invoice arrives for a product or service you never ordered, sometimes with a "call this
number to cancel" instruction designed to get you on the phone with a scammer.

## Overpayment / fake check scams

A buyer sends a payment larger than what was agreed (often via a check that later bounces) and
asks you to refund the difference. You end up sending real money before the original payment is
revealed as fraudulent.

## Fake tech support payment requests

A pop-up or call claims your device has a critical problem and asks for payment to "fix" it
immediately — often combined with a request for remote access to your computer, which should
always be treated with extreme caution.

## Romance and relationship-based payment requests

Someone you've been chatting with online, but haven't met in person, asks for money for an
emergency, travel costs, or a "guaranteed" investment.

## How to protect yourself

- Never pay with gift cards, no matter who is asking or how urgent it seems.
- Verify any unexpected invoice by contacting the company directly through a number/email you
  already trust, not one provided in the message.
- Be suspicious of any payment request tied to unusual urgency.
